# Primer 5 — Interview Toolkit (added beyond the four requested primers)

**Purpose:** Turn the knowledge in Primers 1 to 4 into interview answers. Contains: how to position a mutual fund marketing background for a wholesale banking RM role, a question bank with answer outlines, one case walkthrough, a numbers cheat sheet, questions to ask the interviewer, and a glossary.
**Not covered here:** ICICI Bank's own current financials, strategy and recent news. These should be read from its latest annual report and quarterly results before the interview.

---

## 1. Positioning the background

### 1.1 What she already has
- **Client-facing sales experience** in financial services, including targets and relationship building.
- **Product knowledge of investments:** debt and equity funds, liquid funds, risk classes, NISM V-A, which connects directly to treasury and cash-surplus conversations with corporate clients.
- **Understanding of markets and investor behaviour,** useful for explaining rates, yields and risk to clients.
- **MBA finance:** financial statements and ratios.

### 1.2 The gap to address honestly
Credit appraisal and lending structure are new. The JD says the bank trains people without banking experience ("bankable individuals, not just bankers"), so the interviewer expects this. The best approach: name the gap, show she has already started closing it (the preparation itself is evidence), and describe how she would learn (shadowing senior RMs, studying credit notes, training programmes).

### 1.3 A possible opening statement (edit to her voice)
"I work in mutual fund distribution at DSP, where I work with institutional and high-net-worth clients on where to place their money. Wholesale banking is the other side of the same client: the same company that parks surplus cash also needs working capital, trade finance and hedging. I want to move from advising on where money goes to building long-term relationships around how a business is funded and run, and I have prepared by studying credit analysis, working capital and the current interest rate and currency environment."

## 2. Question bank with answer outlines

### A. About the role and motivation
1. **Why wholesale banking and why ICICI?** Link: corporate relationships, the "One Bank, One Team" 360° approach, exposure to many industries, and training. Avoid generic answers; mention one thing she has read about the bank.
2. **Why move from mutual funds?** Move from product distribution to a full relationship and credit judgment, from selling one product to solving a client's whole set of needs.
3. **What does an RM actually do?** Use Primer 1, section 3: originate, appraise, structure, sanction, document, monitor, cross-sell.
4. **Are you comfortable with role and location changes?** Be clear and honest; the JD states fungibility.
5. **What does "Fair to Customer, Fair to Bank" mean to you?** Primer 1, section 8, plus a real example from her own work where she recommended the right product over the highest-fee one.

### B. Banking mechanics
6. **How does a bank make money?** Spread on loans (NII) plus fees plus float plus treasury. Give the ₹100-crore example from Primer 1.
7. **What is CASA and why does it matter?** Cheap funding; corporate current accounts are the main source for wholesale banking.
8. **Explain CRR and SLR.** Cash with RBI and government securities; both reduce the share of deposits available for lending.
9. **What is an NPA?** Unpaid more than 90 days; SMA stages before that.
10. **What is the difference between funded and non-funded facilities?** Cash out vs a promise to pay.
11. **What is an LC? Walk me through one.** Buyer, seller, issuing bank, documents, payment. Use the German machine example.
12. **What is a bank guarantee, and what are the types?**
13. **How is a corporate loan priced?** Benchmark plus spread, reflecting credit rating, tenor, security and relationship.
14. **What is a covenant? Give examples.** Primer 1, section 5.4.
15. **What is drawing power?** Primer 2, section 7.3.

### C. Financial analysis
16. **Walk me through how you would analyse a company asking for a ₹100 crore loan.** Use the case in section 3 below.
17. **Which ratios matter most for a term loan? For working capital?** DSCR, leverage (TOL/TNW, net debt/EBITDA) and ICR for term loans; current ratio, debtor/inventory/creditor days and the cash conversion cycle for working capital.
18. **A company's profit is rising but its cash flow is falling. What could be happening?** Receivables or inventory building, aggressive revenue recognition, capitalised costs, related-party sales.
19. **What is EBITDA, and what are its limits?** It ignores capital spending, working capital and tax; compare with operating cash flow.
20. **What is DSCR and what value would make you comfortable?** Above 1.25 to 1.5, tested under stress.
21. **How do you assess management quality?** Track record, integrity, transparency, governance, promoter pledging, related-party dealings, reputation with other lenders and suppliers.
22. **What are some early warning signs of a borrower in trouble?** Primer 2, section 9.2.

### D. Markets and instruments
23. **Difference between a T-bill, a CP and a CD?** Government / corporate / bank; all discount instruments up to one year; risk and yield rise in that order.
24. **What is a G-sec yield and why does it matter to banks?** It is the base rate for pricing; banks hold G-secs; yield rises hit their bond books.
25. **Why do bond prices fall when yields rise?** Use the ₹93.29 example.
26. **Liquid fund vs a current account for a company's surplus cash?** Liquid funds have higher expected return; a current account gives instant access and is operationally necessary; the treasury policy balances them. The bank earns float on the account.
27. **What is duration?** Sensitivity of a bond or fund to interest rate changes.
28. **What would you tell a corporate client about where to park ₹20 crore for 45 days?** Options: overnight/liquid fund, T-bills, CDs, short FD; compare safety, yield, liquidity, tax.

### E. Current affairs (Primer 3)
29. **What is happening with the rupee and why?** Oil, foreign selling, strong dollar; RBI intervention; ranges.
30. **What do you expect the RBI to do in October and why?** Inflation at 4.82% and rising, weak rupee, US Fed hike, but growth strong. Market expects a 25 bps hike. Give your view with reasons and acknowledge the alternative (hold).
31. **What is FCNR(B) and why did the RBI open a swap window?** Section 7.3 of Primer 3.
32. **How do rising US yields affect India?** FPI outflows, weaker rupee, higher Indian yields, lower equity valuations.
33. **How would a weaker rupee affect three of your client types?** Exporter gains; importer loses; ECB borrower loses unless hedged.
34. **Why are gold prices up so much over the year, and why did they fall recently?** Safe-haven demand and weaker rupee vs Fed hike and higher real yields.
35. **What happens to bank margins when rates rise?** Repo-linked loans reprice fast; deposits reprice slowly; net effect usually positive at first, but bond portfolios lose value and credit quality may weaken.

### F. Situational and behavioural
36. **A client wants to buy a complex derivative you think is unsuitable. What do you do?** Explain the risk, propose a simpler hedge, involve compliance and the product team, document the discussion; do not sell something harmful.
37. **Your target is behind and a borderline credit comes to you. What do you do?** Do not bend the standards; present it honestly, including the risks, to Risk; look for a safer structure.
38. **A long-standing client's account shows delays and falling turnover. What do you do?** Meet the client, ask for explanations and data, tell your senior and Risk early.
39. **Tell me about a time you handled a difficult client.**
40. **How do you handle working under senior guidance while being expected to bring in business?**

## 3. A worked case: "Sharma Precision wants ₹100 crore"

**Step 1. Clarify the ask.** ₹60 crore cash credit and ₹40 crore LC limit. What for? (Raw material imports and working capital.) Who else lends to them? Why do they want us?

**Step 2. Understand the business.** Auto components supplier to a few car makers. Key questions: customer concentration, dependence on one OEM, raw material price sensitivity (steel), import content, order book, capacity use, competition, technology changes (electric vehicles).

**Step 3. Analyse the financials** (use Primer 2): growth 17.6%; EBITDA margin 17.5%; ICR 3.3x; DSCR 2.0x; net debt/EBITDA 2.3x; current ratio 1.27; cash conversion cycle 124 days and lengthening. The issue is working capital intensity.

**Step 4. Check the management and group.** Promoters' background, other businesses, related-party dealings, pledge of shares, past defaults or disputes, credit bureau and CRILC data.

**Step 5. Assess the request.** Is ₹60 crore of cash credit consistent with the working capital gap and other banks' limits? Does it exceed permissible levels? Would LC usage match the import plan?

**Step 6. Structure.** Security (hypothecation of stock and receivables; collateral), covenants (DSCR, TOL/TNW, current ratio, no more debt), monitoring (monthly stock and debtor statements, account routing), pricing.

**Step 7. Profitability.** Show the total relationship return (Primer 1, section 6).

**Step 8. Risks and mitigants.** OEM concentration (mitigant: long-term supply contracts); commodity price (price pass-through clauses); rupee (hedging policy); slowing auto demand (stress test).

**Step 9. Recommendation.** Approve with conditions, or decline, or reduce.

**What the interviewer listens for:** Does she start with purpose and repayment source, not security? Does she ask about the cash cycle? Does she mention risk and covenants? Can she link numbers to business logic?

## 5. Answer frameworks
- **For any "explain X" question:** definition in one line, a simple example, why it matters to a bank or client, one risk.
- **For any "what will happen" question:** state the view, give two reasons, name what would change your mind.
- **For any numbers question:** show the formula, then the calculation, then what the result says.
- **For behavioural questions:** situation, what she did, result, what she learned.

## 7. Glossary (A to Z, short)
- **Accrual:** interest earned over time.
- **AT1:** Additional Tier 1 capital bond of a bank.
- **BG:** Bank guarantee.
- **CAD:** Current account deficit.
- **CASA:** Current account and savings account deposits.
- **CD:** Certificate of deposit.
- **CP:** Commercial paper.
- **CRR:** Cash reserve ratio.
- **DSCR:** Debt service coverage ratio.
- **ECB:** External commercial borrowing.
- **FCNR(B):** Foreign currency non-resident (bank) deposit.
- **FPI:** Foreign portfolio investor.
- **G-sec:** Government security.
- **LC:** Letter of credit.
- **MCLR:** Marginal cost of funds based lending rate.
- **MPBF:** Maximum permissible bank finance.
- **NCD:** Non-convertible debenture.
- **NII / NIM:** Net interest income / margin.
- **NPA:** Non-performing asset.
- **OIS:** Overnight index swap.
- **PTC:** Pass-through certificate.
- **RAROC:** Risk-adjusted return on capital.
- **Repo:** Sale of security with agreement to repurchase; also RBI's policy rate.
- **RWA:** Risk-weighted assets.
- **SDF / MSF:** Standing deposit facility / marginal standing facility.
- **SDL:** State development loan.
- **SLR:** Statutory liquidity ratio.
- **SMA:** Special mention account.
- **T-bill:** Treasury bill.
- **TOL/TNW:** Total outside liabilities to tangible net worth.
- **VRRR:** Variable rate reverse repo.
- **WACC:** Weighted average cost of capital.
- **YTM:** Yield to maturity.

### 2.1 "Our Approach" (AGM, slide 3): the thinking behind the JD
| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **Principles** | (1) Return of capital (2) **Fair to Customer, Fair to Bank** (3) **One Bank, One Team** (4) Agile risk management (5) Compliance with conscience |
| **Coverage** | **Customer 360°**, built on **micromarkets**, **ecosystems**, and **collaborations and partnerships**; "taking the entire bank to the customer" |
| **Delivery framework** | Focus on quality; process decongestion; "Bank to BankTech" |
| **Anchors** | Integrity, execution, simplify |
| **Goal** | Maximise profit before tax (excluding treasury); "to be the trusted financial service provider" |

How it maps to the RM role: *micromarkets* = picking local/industry clusters to cover; *ecosystems* = the anchor company's suppliers, dealers and employees (Primer 1, section 4.3); *collaborations* = working with fintechs and other partners; *agile risk management* = the credit discipline in the JD. "Return of capital" is worth mentioning: it puts getting the money back ahead of earning on it.

| PBT excl. treasury | 297.06 | 544.79 | 607.13 | 650.21 |

- Over four years PBT excluding treasury rose about 2.2 times, roughly 22% a year (my calculation).

- Wholesale was the largest segment in FY2026: 37.0% of total PBT, against 35.1% for Retail and 26.0% for Treasury. In Q1-2027 it was 39.1%.
- Wholesale profit grew 38.8% y-o-y and 6.3% over the previous quarter.
- The decks do not say how Business Banking profit is split between segments. Check before stating where it is reported.
- "Treasury" segment PBT (₹48.22 bn) is much bigger than "treasury income" in the P&L (₹1.51 bn, slide 8) because they are defined differently. The bank's own target is profit *excluding treasury* (AGM slide 3).

### 3.2 Who counts as "corporate"? (Q1, slides 4, 14)
- **Business Banking** = borrowers with turnover **up to ₹7.50 bn (₹750 crore)**. Portfolio ₹3,501.56 bn, +28.2% y-o-y, 21.4% of advances.
- **Domestic corporate and others** = larger borrowers. Portfolio ₹3,266.31 bn, +18.5% y-o-y, 6.9% over the previous quarter, 19.9% of advances.
- Including non-fund exposure (LC, BG), retail is 41.1% of the total portfolio, so non-retail is about 59%.
- **Consequence for our earlier examples:** the fictional "Sharma Precision" (₹400 crore sales) would be a Business Banking client at ICICI, not a wholesale client. Keep it as a teaching example, but in the interview describe wholesale clients as larger groups, MNCs, PSUs, financial institutions, builders and so on.

## 7. Interview questions with model answers
The answers are written in the first person. She should put them in her own words, keep the numbers, and say "as of the June 2026 quarter".

### Section A: The bank and its strategy

**Q1. What do you know about ICICI Bank?**
"ICICI is the second largest private-sector bank in India by assets and one of the three banks the RBI treats as systemically important. At 30 June 2026 it had deposits of about ₹18.3 lakh crore and loans of about ₹16.3 lakh crore. In the June quarter profit after tax was ₹148 billion, up about 16%, with a net interest margin of 4.36% and a return on assets of 2.49%. Asset quality is good: the net NPA ratio is 0.35%. Capital is strong, with a CET1 ratio of about 16.2%. It also has a group of subsidiaries in insurance, asset management, broking, and housing finance."

**Q2. The JD mentions 'Fair to Customer, Fair to Bank' and 'One Bank, One Team'. Where do these come from?**
"They are two of the five principles in the bank's published approach: return of capital, fair to customer and fair to bank, one bank one team, agile risk management, and compliance with conscience. The bank pairs them with a coverage model built on micromarkets, ecosystems, and partnerships that gives a 360-degree view of each customer, and it says its aim is to take the entire bank to the customer. For an RM that means looking at the whole client relationship, including suppliers, employees, and promoters, and not selling a product the client does not need."

**Q3. How has the bank's profit grown, and is it slowing?**
"Profit before tax excluding treasury rose from about ₹297 billion in FY2022 to ₹650 billion in FY2026, around 22% a year. Growth slowed in FY2026 to about 7% for that measure, and return on equity fell from 18.7% to 16%, partly because the bank built up capital: net worth rose 41% in two years. In the June 2026 quarter growth picked up again to about 21%. One caution is that the 21% figure excludes treasury; total profit before tax grew 13%, because treasury income fell."

**Q4. Why does the bank focus on profit excluding treasury?**
"Treasury income depends on market moves, such as bond prices, and can swing from quarter to quarter: it was ₹12.4 billion in June 2025, negative ₹1.1 billion in March 2026, and ₹1.5 billion in June 2026. Profit from customer business is the steadier measure of how well the bank is serving customers, and that is where an RM contributes."

### Section B: The wholesale business

**Q5. How is the wholesale business doing?**
"The wholesale segment earned ₹244.9 billion of profit before tax in FY2026, which was the largest of the segments, about 37% of the total. In the June 2026 quarter it earned ₹74.8 billion, up 39% on a year earlier and 6% on the previous quarter. Domestic corporate loans were ₹3.27 lakh crore, up 18.5% year on year. So the segment is growing faster than the bank as a whole."

**Q6. What do you notice in the rating mix of the corporate book?**
"About 72% of the corporate book is rated A minus or above on the bank's internal scale. The share rated AA minus or above has fallen from 46% in March 2022 to 27%, and BBB-rated borrowers have risen from 19% to 27%. However, the weak end is almost gone: BB-and-below and non-performing loans together are under half a percent. My reading is that the bank is lending more to solid mid-rated companies, while the very top-rated names may prefer bond markets where spreads are thin. The bank does not explain the trend in the slides, so I would be interested to hear how the team thinks about it."

**Q7. How concentrated is the loan book?**
"Not very. The top 20 borrowers are 6.5% of total exposure and the top 10 groups are 10.1%. All of the top 20 borrowers are internally rated A minus or above. Concentration in the top 20 has fallen from 9.6% in 2022. The top 20 depositors are also only 3.6% of deposits."

**Q8. Which sectors matter most in the book, and which would you watch given the oil shock?**
"Retail finance is about 35% of total exposure. Among non-retail, the largest are finance companies at 7.6%, trade at 6.9%, non-finance services at 4.8%, banks at 4.6%, electronics and engineering at 3.9%, and construction at 3.2%. Crude oil, refining and petrochemicals is 2.9%, down from 4.1% in 2022. With oil near $100, I would look at refiners and petrochemicals, airlines and paint makers, and at builders because higher interest rates can slow sales."

**Q9. NBFCs and builders are big exposures. Why do they matter and how are they performing?**
"NBFCs and housing finance companies are ₹920 billion and builders ₹747 billion, so together they are over ₹1.6 lakh crore. NBFCs depend on wholesale funding, so they suffer if liquidity dries up or rates rise; builders depend on housing demand and approvals. The bank reports under 0.5% of the NBFC/HFC portfolio as BB-and-below or non-performing, and 0.7% of the builder portfolio."

**Q10. Is the corporate book producing more bad loans?**
"In the June 2026 quarter, corporate and business banking added ₹12.2 billion of gross NPAs against ₹10.5 billion a year earlier, while recoveries and upgrades were ₹6.4 billion. In comparison, retail and rural added ₹43.3 billion. So corporate was about a fifth of new NPAs. The corporate BB-and-below watch-list is ₹34.9 billion, about 0.2% of loans. I would keep an eye on the gradual increase in corporate additions, but the level is low."

**Q11. What is the difference between Business Banking and corporate at ICICI?**
"Business Banking covers borrowers with turnover up to ₹750 crore and has grown fastest, up 28% year on year to ₹3.5 lakh crore. The bank says it uses programme-based lending, collateral, and strong monitoring, with about 65% of the portfolio in tickets below ₹10 crore. Corporate covers larger borrowers, where appraisal is customised. A wholesale RM works with the larger clients."

**Q12. What does the overseas wholesale book do?**
"It is US$4.26 billion; 95% is Indian corporates and their overseas subsidiaries or joint ventures, and 4% is foreign companies with Indian links. The overseas margin is only 1.0% against 4.45% domestically, so it is not a profit engine; I read it as a way of serving Indian clients abroad, for example in trade and overseas acquisitions. That is an interpretation, not something the deck states."

### Section C: Funding, rates and capital

**Q13. Why do operating accounts from corporate clients matter to the bank?**
"They are low-cost funding. CASA is 39.5% of deposits, and current accounts are 13.5%, about ₹2.5 lakh crore. Loans have grown 19.6% against deposits at 14.0%, and the credit-deposit ratio has risen from 83.8% to 87.5% in a year. Term deposits, which cost more, grew 17.3%. A corporate client who runs collections and payments through us adds balances that lower our funding cost."

**Q14. If the RBI raises the repo rate next month, what happens to ICICI?**
"About 57% of the domestic loan book is linked to the repo rate or other external benchmarks, so loan yields will reset upwards quickly. Deposits usually reprice more slowly, which can help margin at first. On the other side, the bank holds ₹4.26 lakh crore of government securities for SLR, and they lose value when yields rise; borrowers face higher interest costs; and credit growth can slow. The bank does not publish a sensitivity in these slides, so I am giving the direction, not a number. In the last year, yield on advances fell 67 basis points and cost of funds fell 51, and the margin held at about 4.35%, which shows the effect of rate moves on both sides."

**Q15. Is the bank well capitalised?**
"Yes. CET1 is 16.19% and total capital adequacy is 16.84% at June 2026, on risk-weighted assets of about ₹19.5 lakh crore. In addition, it holds ₹131 billion of contingency provisions (0.8% of advances) above normal provisions, and its provision coverage is 74.7%. As one of three systemically important banks, it needs to hold more capital than smaller banks."

**Q16. The bank's foreign ratings are BBB and Baa3. What does that mean?**
"S&P rates it BBB/Stable and Moody's Baa3/Stable. S&P's view of the bank on its own is 'a minus'. The ratings are at the lowest investment-grade step, which I understand is tied to India's sovereign rating, so they limit how cheaply the bank can borrow abroad. I would say this is my understanding, not something the deck explains."

### Section D: Group and cross-sell

**Q17. How does the group help a wholesale RM?**
"It lets the RM offer the whole bank: the asset management arm, insurance, broking, the primary dealership for government securities, the housing finance company, and ICICI Venture for private equity clients. The primary dealership, for instance, can help with treasury or bond-related needs. Fee income is growing faster than interest income: ₹72.9 billion in the quarter, up 23.5%, against 12.7% for net interest income."

**Q18. You work at DSP, a competitor to ICICI Prudential AMC. How do you see that?**
"ICICI Prudential AMC is the market leader in equity and equity-hybrid funds, with about 14% and 27% market share, so I know the category well from the other side. In wholesale banking my job would be different: advising the client on funding, cash management and risk, and bringing in group products where they suit the client. My fund background helps me talk with corporate treasurers about surplus cash, and I would follow the bank's product and conflict rules."

### Section E: Testing judgement

**Q19. The bank's slides say profit before tax excluding treasury grew 21%, but the total grew only 13%. Why?**
"Treasury income fell from ₹12.4 billion to ₹1.5 billion. So the headline figure, which leaves treasury out, is higher. Profit after tax grew 16%, more than PBT, partly because the effective tax rate fell from about 24.6% to 22.6% in my calculation. It is a reminder to check what each growth number includes."

**Q20. Two of the bank's decks show different non-interest income for FY2026 (₹295.6 bn vs ₹307.6 bn). Which is right?**
"Both. The debt investor deck includes treasury income of ₹11.98 bn in non-interest income, and the quarterly review leaves it out (295.60 + 11.98 = 307.58). It is another example of checking definitions before comparing."

**Q21. If you were the bank, what would worry you in these numbers?**
"Three things. First, loans are growing faster than deposits, so funding is getting tighter and costlier. Second, the corporate book has shifted toward lower internal ratings, although it is still mostly A and above. Third, the environment: high oil, a weak rupee, and a likely rate hike raise costs for borrowers, including builders and NBFCs, which are large exposures. Against that, asset quality, capital and provisions are strong."
